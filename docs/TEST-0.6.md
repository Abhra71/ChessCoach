# Knightwise 0.6 (with 0.7 to 0.9 inside): what to test

After Round 2 of 0.5.0. About 60 minutes. Claude says each step out loud and in the chat, one at a time. It needs
your free Groq key (Round 2, part 6) for parts 1 and 2.

## 1. Talk with your coach, in the app, 8 minutes
| Step | Do | Expect |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Open the coach's box, press **🎙 Talk**, and just say "Hi Maya, how am I doing?" | She hears you in about half a second, answers out loud, then listens again |
| 1.2 | Say "Review my game against" and an opponent's name from your list | The review of that game opens |
| 1.3 | Say "next" a few times | Each step of the review, said out loud |
| 1.4 | Say "Rematch it" on a game you lost | The rematch opens |
| 1.5 | Say nothing for 8 seconds | She stops listening: "I'll be here" |

## 2. Talk on chess.com, 5 minutes
| Step | Do | Expect |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | chess.com home: press **🎙 Talk** in Maya's box | The first time, "Allow the microphone": a Knightwise page opens, press Allow |
| 2.2 | Talk again and ask her something | Answer out loud, listening again |
| 2.3 | Leave the page | The microphone stops |

## 3. Your level, 8 minutes
| Step | Do | Expect |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Sidebar → **Your level** | Five levels; yours open, with each skill's number from your last 20 games and its goal |
| 3.2 | Press a skill's button | Its training opens (practice, a lesson, the endgame trainer or a drill) |
| 3.3 | Is this you? | Say which numbers feel right or wrong |

## 4. The trainers, 20 minutes
| Step | Do | Expect |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Your level → **Endgame trainer** → Mate with king and queen | A fresh position, the idea, a move limit; the defence is perfect |
| 4.2 | Stalemate it on purpose | "Stalemate" with the tip; Take back works |
| 4.3 | Mate it | Confetti, solved 1× |
| 4.4 | **See it in your head** | The board stays still: put in your move, their answer, your next move as arrows, then Check |
| 4.5 | **Your repertoire** | Three lines: as White, against 1.e4, against 1.d4. Practise one |
| 4.6 | Progress → **Slow-down drill** | Your own positions you played in a rush; tick the checks before moving |

## 5. Prep and feedback, 5 minutes
| Step | Do | Expect |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Games → **Prep for an opponent**, type a username | Their openings as White and Black, how their games end, and your score against their start |
| 5.2 | On chess.com, open someone's profile | Maya offers "Prep against them"; on your own profile she isn't there |
| 5.3 | Sidebar → **Send feedback** | Three questions, saved |

## 6. The teacher: the why and Your course, 10 minutes
| Step | Do | Expect |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Games → open a game you lost → step through a key moment | The last step says *why*, from the board: e.g. "Your rook on a8 was already under attack, and g6 did nothing about it" |
| 6.2 | At the end of the moment, **Learn it** | That idea's lesson opens |
| 6.3 | Sidebar → **Your course** | Five levels of lessons; "Next for you" at the top with your number and its goal; some marked "You already do this" |
| 6.4 | Open the next lesson → **Practise it** | Your own positions with that mistake, then Lichess puzzles on the same idea |
| 6.5 | On chess.com, review a game to the last step | **Learn it** opens the lesson in Knightwise |
| 6.6 | Is this you? | Say which reasons felt right or wrong. Wrong ones are the most useful |

Not in this version: studying strong players' games. Lichess's database of master games now needs a Lichess account
(a parent's decision), or a large download; Claude asks first.
